House Price Prediction: Supervised ML Pipeline

An end-to-end supervised regression pipeline in Python that predicts residential sale prices from structured tabular data, built to prioritize leakage-free preprocessing, interpretable feature engineering, and honest model comparison rather than single-score tuning.

Project Specifications

RoleSole Data Scientist & ML Engineer
TaskSupervised regression: predict residential sale price
DatasetRoughly 1,460 records, 80 raw attributes
Pipeline Frameworkscikit-learn Pipeline and ColumnTransformer

Project Overview

A comparative machine learning study evaluating linear and ensemble regression models on a residential sales dataset of roughly 1,460 records and 80 raw attributes. The goal was not to maximize a single leaderboard metric but to establish a defensible methodology: reproducible preprocessing, fold-safe feature engineering, and evaluation on a held-out test set that never informed training or hyperparameter selection.

The pipeline is structured as a scikit-learn Pipeline and ColumnTransformer so that every transform is fit only on training folds, eliminating the target leakage that typically inflates results in ad-hoc notebooks.

Data Preprocessing

  • Separated predictors into numeric and categorical streams using ColumnTransformer, applying each transform only within the training fold.
  • Imputed missing numeric values with median and missing categorical values with an explicit "None" level rather than mode, preserving the information that absence of a feature (for example, no basement, no alley access) is itself meaningful.
  • Addressed outliers by examining the interquartile range on sale price and above-grade living area, then winsorizing extreme tails instead of deleting rows, since removal biased the model against high-value properties.
  • Encoded ordinal quality ratings (overall condition, kitchen quality, exterior quality) with an explicit rank mapping to preserve ordering, and applied one-hot encoding to nominal variables.
  • Applied a log transform to the skewed target variable and to right-skewed square footage features, which reduced heteroscedasticity in the residuals and stabilized error variance across price ranges.

Feature Engineering

  • Constructed total finished square footage as the sum of basement, first-floor, and second-floor areas, correcting for properties where the raw columns double-counted or omitted below-grade space.
  • Built total bathroom count as a weighted composite (full baths + 0.5 × half baths) to produce a single continuous amenity feature.
  • Derived age at sale (year sold minus year built) and years since remodel, then created an interaction term between overall quality and above-grade living area to capture the compounding effect of size and finish quality on valuation.
  • Generated neighborhood-level aggregate price statistics using fold-safe target encoding, computing statistics exclusively on training folds to prevent leakage into validation and test data.
  • Engineered a garage and parking composite feature to consolidate several sparse, correlated columns into a single signal.

Modeling & Evaluation

  • Established a Linear Regression baseline, then extended it with Ridge and Lasso regularization to quantify how much of the ensemble's advantage came from nonlinearity versus simple variance reduction.
  • Trained Random Forest and Gradient Boosting regressors with hyperparameters tuned via GridSearchCV over n_estimators, max_depth, learning_rate, and min_samples_leaf, using 5-fold cross-validation on the training split only.
  • Evaluated on a 20 percent held-out test set using RMSE, MAE, and R². Reported RMSE on the original price scale after back-transforming from log space so the error is interpretable in dollars.
  • Diagnosed underfitting and overfitting by comparing training and cross-validation error curves against max_depth and n_estimators, which showed the Random Forest saturating early while Gradient Boosting continued to improve at lower learning rates.
  • Generated feature-importance visualizations in matplotlib, cross-checking impurity-based importance against permutation importance to avoid over-trusting high-cardinality features.
  • Produced residual plots and a predicted-versus-actual scatter plot to verify that error was homoscedastic and not systematically worse at the price extremes.

Results

Gradient Boosting achieved the strongest held-out performance, with R² near 0.90 and RMSE in the low 20,000 dollar range, outperforming the regularized linear baseline by a meaningful margin on the same split. Permutation importance consistently ranked overall quality, total finished square footage, and neighborhood target encoding as the dominant predictors, which matched domain intuition and served as a sanity check on the pipeline.
